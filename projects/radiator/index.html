Radiator

These cars run hot, they're old, tired, and most importantly, the front end doesn't really let them breathe very well. In fact, there's a small plastic deflector on the front, and it doesn't do much, but it also does everything. Remove it and see how well your Camaro performs.

Another issue with some of these cars is the 3 blade fan, and the tiny radiator. Maybe these worked well when everything was new, but things wear down and efficiency is lost, so they're inadequate now.

At some point, someone swapped out the original radiator with this workhorse. It's a better radiator, but it's at the end of its life. While looking for a suitable replacement radiator, I came to discover that they come in many shapes and sizes, requiring different coolant hoses, and possibly also needing coolant lines to be modified. Thankfully, after much searching, I found this, the exact radiator I'm already rocking. more here

Removing the radiator is pretty straight forward. It sits in a trough on rubber isolators and is held in place by a fan shroud at the top. My non-ac 305 doesn't get a full shroud, it gets a half shroud. Removing 4 bolts with a 10mm socket exposes the top of the radiator. The stock radiator hose clamps can be removed with a flathead screwdriver, and that leaves only the transmission coolant lines. These metal lines are best removed with the correct flare nut wrench. Be prepared to catch some transmission fluid that will definitely leak from the rad and the lines.
