Fuel Pump

Based on the beat up and corroded condition of the existing fuel pump, and other parts on this car, I can only speculate that it's the original pump from 1984. Sure, it was pumping just fine, but it was also weeping, it was probably tired, and I was already doing the fuel lines.

On the 305ci LG4 of this era, the fuel pump is mechanical, and located on the front right side of the engine. The front of the camshaft has a special lobe that activates a pushrod. The pump is just a diaphragm inside of an enclosure, and that pushrod fights a spring causing the diaphragm to pulsate (pump).

Changing it seems pretty straightforward, just 3 fuel lines and two bolts... but not so fast!!... Remember that pushrod? When you remove the fuel pump, that pushrod slides down occupying the space where the pump lever once was. When you install the new pump, the pushrod will be in your way. So just move it you say... yeah, good luck with that. It's a slippery little thing, and there's not much space to work, but there is another way.

On the front of the engine, you'll find a small bolt that seems to serve no purpose. Even removing the bolt tells you nothing. The removal of that bolt opens a passageway that lines up with the side of the fuel pump pushrod. If you position the number one cylinder at top dead center of the compression stroke, remove the mysterious bolt, and thread a longer bolt in its place, that bolt will stop the pushrod from sliding down. Now you can safely remove and reinstall the fuel pump without having to fidget around with the pushrod. Here's a tip, you can even use one of the fuel pump bolt.
